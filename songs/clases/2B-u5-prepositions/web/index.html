English 2B · Unit 5 · Welcome to My Town! · Week 2

Where Is Teddy?

Prepositions of place: in, on, behind, in front of, next to, between.

Lesson flow · 45 minutes

Same structure as the Toddle plan. New pieces are the song, the on-screen cards and the guessing game.

  1. 5'Warm-up

    The plushie hides

    Students on the rug. Put the class plushie in, on, behind, in front of and next to the box, and between the box and the basket. Ask each time: Where is our plushie? Accept pointing first, then words.
  2. 8'Present

    Meet the six words new

    Open 1 · Learn. Tap each card: Teddy says the sentence and the class repeats it with the gesture. Then move to the Canva presentation and do its exercises together.

    Order matters: in and on first, between last, because it needs two objects.

  3. 8'Song

    Where Is Teddy? new

    Open 2 · Song. Play it three times: listen and point, then sing with the gestures, then sing standing up. In each verse Teddy is hidden while the class sings "Where is Teddy?", so they can guess before he appears.
  4. 7'Practice

    Guess where Teddy is new

    Open 3 · Where is Teddy?. One student comes to the board, says the full sentence ("Teddy is behind the box") and then taps the answer.
  5. 12'Notebook

    Anchor chart and worksheet

    Hand out the anchor chart to stick in the notebook. Students complete the worksheet on their own while you walk around and ask "Where is the…?"
  6. 5'Closure

    Simon says

    Open 4 · Simon says and read the commands aloud. Some have no "Simon says": whoever moves sits down.

Objective

Students name and use six prepositions of place to say where an object or person is: "Teddy is next to the basket."

Materials

  • Class plushie, a box and a basket
  • Canva presentation (Toddle resource)
  • Anchor chart and worksheet
  • This page on the projector, with sound

Check for understanding

  • Can point to the right place when they hear the word
  • Can say a full sentence: Teddy is … the box.
  • Distinguishes behind from in front of (the usual mix-up)

Link to the project

The song's bridge moves to the town: the park is next to the bakery, the school is behind the big green tree. Students can describe the ideal town they drew last week the same way.
